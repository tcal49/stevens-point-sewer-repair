Common Sewer Problems

One slow sink may be a small drain problem. Several fixtures acting up together can point to the main sewer line. Choose the symptom that best matches what is happening.

Describe Your Sewer Problem

Multiple Drains Backing Up

When several fixtures back up together, a restriction in the main sewer line may be preventing wastewater from leaving the home.

Understand this problem

Gurgling Toilets

Bubbling or gurgling from a toilet can signal trapped air caused by a partial drain blockage, main-line restriction, or venting problem.

Understand this problem

Sewage Odor

Persistent sewer-gas smells may come from a dry trap, failed seal, venting problem, backup, or opening in the sewer system.

Understand this problem

Basement Drain Backups

Wastewater at a basement floor drain often means a downstream restriction is forcing flow out through the home's lowest drain opening.

Understand this problem

Pooling Water or Sewage

Standing wastewater indoors, near a cleanout, or above the sewer route can indicate a backup, leaking lateral, or failed pipe connection.

Understand this problem

Green or Soggy Yard

A persistently wet, soft, or unusually green strip above the sewer route can be a clue that an underground lateral is leaking.

Understand this problem

Recurring Clogs

A drain or sewer blockage that repeatedly returns may indicate roots, buildup, a pipe defect, or incomplete clearing rather than a one-time clog.

Understand this problem

Slow Drains Throughout Your Home

Several slow fixtures at once can indicate that the main sewer line is becoming restricted before a complete blockage or backup occurs.

Understand this problem

Frozen Sewer Line

A frozen sewer or drain line can suddenly stop flow during prolonged cold, causing gurgling, slow drainage, or wastewater to back up through low fixtures.

Understand this problem

Straight answers

Stevens Point Sewer Repair FAQs

Quick answers to the questions homeowners often ask before scheduling a sewer inspection or repair.

How do I know whether the sewer line is the problem?+

A main-line problem often affects more than one drain at the same time. Repeated backups, gurgling toilets, sewer odors, or wastewater at a basement floor drain are common clues. A camera inspection can help confirm the location and cause.

Does every damaged sewer line need to be replaced?+

No. Some problems can be cleaned or repaired in one section. Replacement is more likely when a pipe is broadly collapsed, badly deteriorated, or has several failures. The right recommendation depends on the inspection findings and pipe condition.

Can a sewer line be repaired without digging up the whole yard?+

Sometimes. Pipe lining and pipe bursting can reduce excavation when the existing route and pipe conditions are suitable. Traditional digging may still be the better or only option for certain collapses, offsets, or access conditions.

Who is responsible for the sewer line in Stevens Point?+

Responsibility commonly depends on where the problem is located and local utility rules. A homeowner is often responsible for the private lateral running from the building toward the public main, but the exact boundary should be confirmed for the property.

What should I do if sewage is backing up right now?+

Stop using water, keep people and pets away from contaminated areas, avoid chemical drain cleaners, and request professional help. If wastewater is near electrical equipment, do not enter the area until it is safe.
